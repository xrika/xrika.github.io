The Problem

My recipes live on four different platforms, three browser tabs, and one very unreliable memory. Dinner shouldn't be this hard!

The User

I. Myself

II. Homecookers who want to spend less time planning meals and more time enjoying them

About This Project

MunchMatch is a collaborative project with my partner. I lead the UI/UX design while he handles development (Android and iOs). It started with my own frustration as the main home cook: successful recipes scattered across platforms, no single place to save them, and a daily mental load that made meal planning feel like a second job.

The core question we kept coming back to was: "How might a swiping-based interface reduce decision paralysis and help home cooks plan meals with less cognitive effort and stress?"

I researched existing recipe apps, including Cookpad, one of Japan's largest recipe-sharing platforms and identified what made them work for users and where they fell short for our specific problem.

That research shaped the product direction. I had a vision of the colour scheme, theme, typography, and overall vibe of the app before I even opened Figma, and that was all thanks to the research phase. It was a crucial step that informed every design decision I made after.

Process

I started with myself as the primary user, mapping my own pain points before expanding to personas that captured the range of home cooks who experience the same friction. From there I built user journey maps, hand-sketched low-fidelity wireframes, and iterated toward a UI that felt light and intuitive; not another complicated app to manage and it HAS to work for me.

The MVP focuses on three things: a swiping interface for recipe cards, a randomise feature for when decision paralysis hits hardest, and a simple recipe creation flow with notes and photo attachments.
